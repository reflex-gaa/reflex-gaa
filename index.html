Over or wide? Let the ball decide.

Reflex GAA is a smart ball that tracks its own position, so umpires can check a score in seconds in any stadium in the country.

Created by Diarmuid Morgan and Rohaan Bhatnagar, St. Patrick’s Classical School

Why we built it

Diarmuid plays GAA for Wolfe Tones. Many of his matches have been tight, with one or two points separating the teams, and too often there have been dodgy calls from the umpires behind the goals.

Hawkeye is only available in Croke Park and Semple Stadium. We thought we could make the same service compact enough to fit inside a ball, so it can be used in any stadium around the country.

Our mission

We want to make the GAA a fairer place to compete. We’ve relied on decisions from umpires standing behind the goal for too long. In all our years of watching football we’ve heard the same comments: “You need to go to Specsavers,” “That was clearly over, ref,” or “That was clearly wide, ref.”

A decision from the umpire can change the course of a game, so it needs to be right.

Inside the Reflex GAA Smart Ball

We add four components to the ball.

UWB module

A compact ultra-wideband module gives precise tracking and fits inside a football without affecting its balance or aerodynamics.

Power supply

A small, lightweight thin-film Li-Po battery. Its capacity has to be enough to power the UWB module for the whole game.

Microcontroller

A small ESP-32 microcontroller works with the UWB module to handle data logging.

Data transfer

Bluetooth Low Energy sends the data to a nearby receiver for real-time analysis during play.

How the system works

  1. 1

    Pre-match

    Before the match, modules are attached to the two goal posts. This lets the posts track the ball’s precise location.

  2. 2

    During the game

    While the ball is moving, its co-ordinates are constantly updated and relayed through the system.

  3. 3

    In action

    If the umpire can’t tell whether a shot was over or wide, he calls on another official who reads the data on a laptop. It shows whether the ball passed between the two posts or went wide.
